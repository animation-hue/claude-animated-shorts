## Voice-over (244 words → ≈65 s)
> At half past nine one morning, at a red light on Marine Drive, Bijli Badshah, the fastest little auto in Mumbai, fell in love.
> Her name was Gulabi Rani. Pink roof. Jasmine on her mirror. Bijli's headlights went wide. His little engine skipped a beat.
> Then the light turned green, and she was gone.
> Natraj, his driver, pulled down his goggles. They followed. Past the sea wall. Round the corner. Into the busiest roundabout in Mumbai, where every auto looks exactly the same.
> When they came out, she wasn't there.
> All day, they searched. A pink roof! A grandma. A pink roof! An umbrella. A pink roof… that drove away.
> Bijli's headlights drooped a little lower every time.
> Then, at twenty past five, back on Marine Drive, he smelled jasmine. There she was, across the road.
> He didn't look left. He didn't look right. He only looked at her. Which is how he met the lamp post.
> In Happy Singh's garage, one cracked headlight and a lot of tape later, Bijli sat alone. Even Happy Singh wasn't happy. Bijli had never even said hello.
> Then the garage door filled with golden light, and someone honked, very softly. It was Gulabi. She had seen everything.
> She hung a jasmine flower on his wobbly mirror.
> Bijli couldn't say a word. So he flickered his one good headlight. Once. Softly. She flickered back.
> And in the golden evening, two autos drove along Marine Drive. Slowly, this time.
