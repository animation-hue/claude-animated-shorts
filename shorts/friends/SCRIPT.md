## Voice-over (≈440 words → ≈180 s at this narrator's pace)

> Ram and Shyam had been best friends since they were five years old.
> They had a secret handshake. Slap. Slap. Bump. Thumbs up. It meant one thing: whatever happens, we stick together.
> One morning, the two friends decided to cross the big jungle to the next village.
> "If there is any danger," said Ram, "we help each other. Promise?"
> "Promise," said Shyam. Slap. Slap. Bump. Thumbs up.
> Ram marched in front with a stick. "I am not scared of anything," he said. "Tigers, snakes, ghosts. Nothing."
> The jungle was quiet. The trees were so tall that the sun came down in thin golden lines.
> Then, something moved in the bushes.
> Ram jumped. It was a rabbit.
> A little later, there was a deep, low growl. Ram jumped higher. It was Shyam's stomach. He had skipped breakfast.
> Then a huge shadow passed over them. Ram jumped so high he nearly lost his shoes. It was a hornbill, looking very unimpressed.
> Shyam laughed. Ram didn't.
> And then the bushes opened, and out stepped a bear. A real one. Big, brown, and very, very close.
> For one second, nobody moved.
> Then Ram dropped his stick, and ran.
> He ran to the nearest tree and climbed it faster than any monkey in the jungle. He didn't look back. Not once.
> Shyam was alone. He couldn't climb. There was no time to run.
> And then he remembered something his grandmother always said. "A bear will never touch someone who is dead."
> So Shyam lay down in the middle of the path. He closed his eyes. And he held his breath.
> The bear came closer. It sniffed his shoes. It sniffed his hand. It sniffed his face.
> Up in the tree, Ram held on tight, and said nothing.
> The bear put its nose right next to Shyam's ear, as if it was whispering something. It stayed there for a long, long time.
> Shyam didn't move. Not a finger. Not an eyelash.
> And then, slowly, the bear turned around, and walked back into the jungle.
> Ram waited until it was really gone. Then he slid down the tree, dusted off his clothes, and smiled.
> "Shyam! You're alive! So, what did the bear whisper in your ear?"
> Shyam stood up. He took a long breath.
> "The bear told me something very important," he said. "Never trust a friend who leaves you alone when danger comes."
> Ram's smile disappeared.
> He held out his hand. Slap. Slap…
> But Shyam didn't finish the handshake. He picked up his bag, and walked home through the golden evening.
> Because a true friend isn't the one who makes promises. It's the one who stays.
